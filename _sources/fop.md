# Food Oral Processing

Food oral processing (FOP) describes the dynamic sequence of mechanical and biochemical transformations that occur when food enters the mouth until swallowing. It bridges food physics, sensory science, and physiology.

## The Oral Processing Chain

The transformation of food in the oral cavity follows a structured sequence:

$$
\text{Food Structure} \xrightarrow{\text{mastication}} \text{Bolus Formation} \xrightarrow{\text{lubrication}} \text{Swallowing}
$$

Each stage involves distinct physical phenomena—particle breakdown, saliva incorporation, and tribological interactions between the bolus and oral surfaces.

## Key Physical Parameters

Oral processing integrates multiple measurable quantities:

| Parameter | Symbol | Relevance |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Viscosity | $\eta$ | Flow behavior during manipulation |
| Friction coefficient | $\mu$ | Surface interactions (tongue-palate) |
| Particle size distribution | $d_{50}$ | Grittiness, smoothness perception |
| Yield stress | $\sigma_y$ | Structural breakdown threshold |
| Adhesion force | $F_a$ | Coating, mouthfeel persistence |

## Why FOP Matters

Understanding oral processing enables:

- Rational design of texture-modified foods for dysphagia patients
- Optimization of plant-based alternatives to match animal product mouthfeel
- Prediction of sensory attributes from instrumental measurements
- Reduction of fat, sugar, and salt while maintaining palatability
