# 1.3 Human Tongue

The tongue is the primary effector organ in food oral processing, executing bolus manipulation, texture assessment, and taste transduction. Its unique biomechanical and sensory properties make it central to both mechanical processing and perception.

## Surface Morphology

### Papillae Types

| Papillae Type | Location | Primary Function |
|---------------|----------|------------------|
| Filiform | Entire dorsum | Mechanical grip, texture sensing |
| Fungiform | Anterior 2/3 | Taste (gustatory), temperature, touch |
| Foliate | Lateral posterior | Taste (gustatory) |
| Circumvallate | Posterior V-line | Taste (gustatory) |

Filiform papillae dominate surface coverage and create the characteristic rough texture critical for food manipulation and tribological contact.

## Biomechanical Properties

The tongue is a muscular hydrostat—a boneless structure capable of complex 3D deformation:

- **Intrinsic muscles**: Control shape (elongation, shortening, curling)
- **Extrinsic muscles**: Control position and gross movement
- **Tissue stiffness**: ~2–5 kPa (soft tissue range)

During oral processing, the tongue exerts forces of 10–50 kPa against the palate, sufficient for soft food compression and bolus manipulation.

## Sensory Innervation

### Mechanoreception

High density of mechanoreceptors (Merkel cells, Meissner corpuscles) enables:

- Spatial resolution: ~1 mm (comparable to fingertips)
- Particle size discrimination: Detection threshold ~10–20 μm
- Texture attribute encoding: Roughness, hardness, viscosity

### Chemoreception

Taste receptor cells in gustatory papillae transduce the five basic tastes (sweet, sour, salty, bitter, umami) plus additional modalities (fat, kokumi).

## Role in Tribological Perception

The tongue–palate interface is the primary site for oral tribology. During thin-film lubrication:

- Tongue surface deformation accommodates fluid film
- Papillae height (~300 μm) influences entrainment
- Salivary pellicle on both surfaces modulates friction

Perceived attributes such as smoothness, creaminess, and astringency correlate with friction coefficients measured at tongue-relevant conditions.

## Implications for Biomimetic Systems

Artificial tongue surfaces in tribometers must replicate:

- Surface roughness (Ra ~20–50 μm)
- Elastic modulus (~2–5 kPa for PDMS substrates)
- Hydration state and boundary layer formation
