# Oral Tribology

Oral tribology studies the friction, lubrication, and textural perception of foods during oral processing. Quantifying the interaction between food, saliva, and oral surfaces, it provides mechanistic insights into sensory attributes such as smoothness, creaminess, and astringency.

## The Stribeck Framework

The Stribeck curve relates the friction coefficient $\mu$ to the Hersey number:

$$
\text{Hersey number} = \frac{\eta \cdot v}{F_n}
$$

Three distinct regimes emerge:

| Regime | Characteristics | Sensory Correlates |
|--------|----------------|-------------------|
| Boundary | Surface asperities in contact | Astringency, roughness |
| Mixed | Partial fluid film | Creaminess transition |
| Hydrodynamic | Full fluid separation | Smoothness, slipperiness |

## Novel Biomimetic Oral Tribometer

This **novel biomimetic oral tribometer** is a custom-designed instrument that mimics tongue-palate interactions under controlled conditions. Key features include:

- **Simulated Oral Surfaces:** Flexible, compliant surfaces that replicate tongue and palate elasticity
- **Controlled Sliding:** Adjustable velocity and normal force to reproduce mastication dynamics
- **Environmental Control:** Temperature and humidity settings matching oral conditions
- **Real-time Friction Measurement:** High-resolution sensors capturing friction coefficient $\mu$ over time

### Typical Workflow

1. **Sample Preparation:** Food or emulsion is prepared under standard laboratory conditions
2. **Mounting on Tribometer:** Sample placed between compliant upper and lower surfaces
3. **Measurement:** Record friction force while varying sliding velocity, normal force, or lubrication
4. **Data Analysis:** Compute friction coefficient and correlate with sensory perception

### Representative Equation

The friction coefficient $\mu$ measured by the tribometer can be expressed as:

$$
\mu = \frac{F_{\text{friction}}}{F_{\text{normal}}} = f(\eta, v, F_n, \sigma, \text{saliva})
$$

Where:

- $\eta$ = viscosity of the sample
- $v$ = sliding velocity
- $F_n$ = normal force
- $\sigma$ = surface roughness
- saliva = presence and composition of lubricating fluid

### Applications

- Quantifying smoothness and creaminess of dairy and plant-based emulsions
- Studying the influence of fat, protein, and hydrocolloids on oral perception
- Supporting predictive models for sensory outcomes in product development
